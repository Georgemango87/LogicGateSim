🚀 Section 1: The Basics (What Is This?)

                        
The big idea

                        
Think of logic gates as Lego bricks for electricity and thinking. Each brick has a tiny job — "are both of my inputs on?" or "flip my input" — and when you snap them together, they can add numbers, remember things, and light up pixels.

                        
The simulator speaks one language: on or off. That's it.

                        

                            
What we sayWhat it meansEveryday picture

                            
                                
1 / High / TrueOn — power is flowing💡 A light switch flipped UP

                                
0 / Low / FalseOff — no power🌑 A light switch flipped DOWN

                            
                        


                        
The core components


                        
🔘 Toggle & Push Buttons — the switches

                        
    
                            
  • Toggle: a light switch. Click it once to turn it on, again to turn it off. It stays where you put it.
    • 
                            
  • Push: a doorbell. It's only on while you're holding it down. Let go and it snaps back off.
    • 
                        


                        
💡 Output & Pixel — the lights

                        
    
                            
  • Output: a light bulb that glows when it receives a 1. It also flashes red if it receives a confused signal (more on that in Section 5).
    • 
                            
  • Pixel: a single resizable square of light. Perfect for drawing screens or showing a color. Drag its bottom-right corner to make it bigger.
    • 
                        


                        
🧠 AND, OR, NOT — the decision makers

                        
    
                            
  • AND: "Both of you, or no deal." It outputs 1 only when all inputs are 1. Like a bank vault that needs two keys turned at the same time. 🗝️🗝️
    • 
                            
  • OR: "Either of you is enough." It outputs 1 when at least one input is 1. Like a room with two doors — walk through either one. 🚪🚪
    • 
                            
  • NOT: "The contrarian." It flips whatever it receives. Give it a 1, it gives you a 0. Give it a 0, it gives you a 1. 🙃
    • 
                        


                        
Bonus gates you'll meet later: XOR (exactly one, not both), NAND (NOT + AND), NOR (NOT + OR), XNOR (NOT + XOR). They're just combinations of the three above — same ideas, different attitudes.

                    


                    
                    

                        
🔌 Section 2: Building Your First Circuit

                        
Let's make a light you can control. It takes three steps. ⚡


                        
Step 1 — Place a switch

                        
    
                            
  1. Find the Toggle button in the top toolbar.
    2. 
                            
  2. Drag it onto the empty canvas (or just click it to spawn one).
    3. 
                            
  3. You now have a switch. It says OFF.
    4. 
                        


                        
Step 2 — Place a light

                        
    
                            
  1. Find the Output button in the toolbar.
    2. 
                            
  2. Drag it onto the canvas, somewhere to the right of your switch.
    3. 
                            
  3. You now have a bulb. It's dark.
    4. 
                        


                        
Step 3 — Connect them

                        
    
                            
  1. Hover over the small round port on the right side of the Toggle.
    2. 
                            
  2. Click and drag from that port to the small round port on the left side of the Output.
    3. 
                            
  3. Let go. A wire appears. 🎉
    4. 
                        

                        
Now click the Toggle. The button flips to ON, the wire turns green, and the bulb glows. Click it again — everything goes back to gray and the bulb goes dark. You just built a working circuit.


                        
How do I know a wire is active?

                        
    
                            
  • Gray = the wire is carrying a 0 (off).
    • 
                            
  • Green = the wire is carrying a 1 (on).
    • 
                            
  • Gray dashed = the wire is floating (nobody is driving it — see "Z" in Section 5).
    • 
                            
  • Red, flashing = two gates are fighting over the same wire (contention), or a wire width doesn't match (see "X" in Section 5).
    • 
                        


                        
How do I delete things quickly?

                        
    
                            
  • Delete a gate: click the little × in its header. Or click the gate to select it and press Delete / Backspace.
    • 
                            
  • Delete a wire: double-click it, or right-click it and choose ✕ Delete wire.
    • 
                            
  • Delete a selection: drag a box around multiple gates with Shift + drag, then press Delete.
    • 
                            
  • Undo anything: Ctrl + Z (or Cmd + Z on Mac). Redo with Ctrl + Shift + Z.
    • 
                        

                        
💡 Tip: You can also select a gate and click the × in its header — that deletes just that gate and any wires attached to it.

                    


                    
                    

                        
⚡ Section 3: Advanced Wiring (Pour Mode)

                        
Normal wiring is one hose, one flower bed: drag from an output to an input, repeat for every connection. Pour Mode is a garden hose with a splitter — you point it at as many beds as you like without reconnecting the tap. 🌱


                        
How to enter Pour Mode

                        
    
                            
  1. Click (don't drag) any output port — the round port on the right side of a gate.
    2. 
                            
  2. The port turns amber/orange and starts to pulse. A 🔌 Pour mode pill appears in the toolbar, and a mode badge shows in the corner. You're in Pour Mode.
    3. 
                        


                        
How to hook up inputs

                        
    
                            
  1. Click any input port — the round port on the left side of a gate.
    2. 
                            
  2. A wire snaps from your chosen output to that input. Done.
    3. 
                            
  3. Click more input ports to wire them all from the same source. No dragging, no re-clicking the output.
    4. 
                            
  4. Each new wire flashes green to confirm.
    5. 
                        


                        
How to exit or cancel

                        
    
                            
  • Press Esc, or
    • 
                            
  • Right-click anywhere, or
    • 
                            
  • Click the same output port again, or
    • 
                            
  • Click the ✕ on the 🔌 Pour mode toolbar pill.
    • 
                        

                        
The badge and the amber glow disappear, and you're back to Standard mode.


                        
Bonus pour trick: wire from selected gates

                        
    
                            
  1. Shift + click several gates to select them (or Shift + drag a box around them).
    2. 
                            
  2. Shift + click an input port on another gate.
    3. 
                            
  3. A wire is created from the next selected source — clicking more inputs cycles through your selected gates in round-robin order. This is the fastest way to wire a big bank of inputs to a row of gates.
    4. 
                        

                        
🖱️ Cursor tooltip: while in Pour Mode, a small tooltip follows your cursor reminding you to "Click inputs to wire · Right-click to cancel". You can turn it off in Settings → ⚡ Preferences → Show the pour-mode tooltip.

                    


                    
                    

                        
⚙️ Section 4: Magic Tricks (Custom Gates & Scripting)

                        
This is where the simulator stops being a toy and starts being a workshop. 🪄


                        
🏗️ Custom Logic / Custom ICs — build once, use everywhere

                        
Think of a custom gate like saving a group of Lego bricks as a brand-new brick. You build a useful sub-circuit once (an adder, a multiplexer, a little logic puzzle), and then you can drop it onto the canvas as a single block — with its own input and output pins — as many times as you want. It's like prefabricating a wall in a workshop and then just slotting it into house after house.


                        
Build a custom gate from scratch

                        
    
                            
  1. Build a circuit on the canvas that has at least one INPUT or PUSH and at least one OUTPUT that's actually wired up.
    2. 
                            
  2. Click ⚙️ Custom Gate in the toolbar.
    3. 
                            
  3. A mini-simulator opens. If you had gates selected on the main canvas, they're already loaded inside — otherwise, drag gates from the mini-toolbar to build your circuit.
    4. 
                            
  4. Name your gate, pick a color, and optionally choose a folder.
    5. 
                            
  5. Click OK. Your gate appears in the toolbar as a new button. Drag it onto the canvas like any other gate. 🎉
    6. 
                        


                        
Edit an existing custom gate

                        
    
                            
  • Right-click a custom gate button (or an instance on the canvas) and choose Edit.
    • 
                            
  • Change anything — name, color, inner logic.
    • 
                            
  • Click OK. Every existing instance is refreshed automatically.
    • 
                            
  • To delete it everywhere, click 🗑 Delete Gate in the modal.
    • 
                        


                        
Organize with folders

                        
    
                            
  • Click the 📁+ button in the toolbar to create a folder. Give it a name and a color.
    • 
                            
  • Drag a custom gate button onto a folder button to move it inside.
    • 
                            
  • Click a folder to open a dropdown of its gates. Drag one out to place it.
    • 
                            
  • Right-click a folder to rename, recolor, or delete it (with or without its contents).
    • 
                        


                        
Truth table → one custom block

                        
    
                            
  1. Click 📋 Logic Table in the toolbar.
    2. 
                            
  2. Choose the number of inputs (up to 8) and outputs (up to 4).
    3. 
                            
  3. Click any output cell to cycle it: 0 → 1 → X. (X means "don't care".)
    4. 
                            
  4. Click Generate Gate → to see a live preview of the minimized circuit.
    5. 
                            
  5. Name it, color it, save it. Done — you have a custom gate built from a truth table.
    6. 
                        

                        
🧠 Under the hood: the simulator runs the Quine–McCluskey algorithm to find the smallest possible sum-of-products circuit for your table. You get the elegant version, automatically.


                        
📜 Custom Scripting — type it, and it appears

                        
The simulator has a secret superpower: you can type a Boolean equation as plain text and it will build the entire circuit for you, laid out and wired. No clicking, no dragging.


                        
Try it

                        
    
                            
  1. Click ⚡ Script in the toolbar. A sidebar slides in from the right.
    2. 
                            
  2. Type an equation, for example: Y = (A AND B) OR NOT C
    3. 
                            
  3. Click ⚡ Compile Circuit.
    4. 
                        

                        
The canvas clears and a fully-wired circuit appears — inputs on the left, gates in the middle, your output(s) on the right. It really is that easy.


                        
The syntax

                        
    
                            
  • Assignment: Name = expression
    • 
                            
  • Operators: AND, OR, NOT, XOR, NAND, NOR, XNOR
    • 
                            
  • Symbols: & (AND), | (OR), ~ or ! (NOT), ^ (XOR)
    • 
                            
  • Parentheses: for grouping, just like math.
    • 
                            
  • Comments: start a line with #
    • 
                            
  • Multi-line: each line is a named signal you can reuse, e.g.
    inputs: A, B, Cin
    Sum  = A XOR B XOR Cin
    Cout = (A AND B) OR (Cin AND (A XOR B))
    • 
                            
  • Optional input list: inputs: A, B, C tells the compiler exactly which names are switches. Any other name on the left side of an = becomes an internal signal, and any name that's never assigned becomes an automatic input.
    • 
                        


                        
What happens when you compile

                        
    
                            
  • The whole circuit is auto-laid out in neat columns — inputs on the left, gates in the middle, outputs on the right.
    • 
                            
  • Identical sub-expressions are shared — write (A AND B) five times and the compiler builds it once and wires it everywhere.
    • 
                            
  • If a circuit already exists, you'll be asked to confirm before it's replaced. The whole build is one undo step.
    • 
                            
  • The status line at the bottom tells you how many inputs, gates, and outputs were created.
    • 
                        

                        
🪄 Pro tip: if you want a reusable version of your scripted circuit, save it as a custom gate. The two features stack beautifully.

🐞 Debug Mode — X-ray vision for your circuit

                        
Press D (or click the 🐞 Debug button) to enter Debug Mode. Now:

                        
    
                            
  • Hover any gate or wire → a dark tooltip shows its live signal values, input states, and internal counters.
    • 
                            
  • Click a gate's body or a wire → pin the tooltip so you can read it while you work.
    • 
                            
  • Esc → unpin.
    • 
                            
  • Unconnected input ports flash red so you can spot loose ends at a glance.
    • 
                            
  • Broken custom gates (missing definitions) get a dashed red outline.
    • 
                            
  • Right-click any port or wire → 📈 Add to Timing Diagram to track its waveform over time.
    • 
                        


                        
📈 Timing Diagram — see signals over time

                        
    
                            
  • Click 📈 Timing in the toolbar to open the waveform panel at the bottom.
    • 
                            
  • Right-click any port or wire → Add to Timing Diagram to start tracking it.
    • 
                            
  • Watch the waveform scroll as your clocks tick. Clock edges automatically trigger a capture, so you can see glitches and propagation delays.
    • 
                            
  • Clear empties the history. ✕ closes the panel.
    •

🔬 Little-known signal truths (the "4-state" model)

                        
Standard logic is 0 and 1 — but real wires can also be floating or fighting. This simulator speaks all four:

                        

                            
ValueMeaningWire color

                            
                                
0Low / offDark

                                
1High / onGreen

                                
ZFloating — nobody is driving this wireGray, dashed

                                
XContention — two gates are fighting over the same wire, or a width mismatchRed, flashing

                            
                        

                        
    
                            
  • Output bulbs glow orange for 1, flash red for X.
    • 
                            
  • Tri-state buffer (TRI): outputs Z when its enable input is 0, passes its data input when enable is 1.
    • 
                            
  • Wire width mismatch: if you connect a 4-bit output to a 1-bit input, the wire flashes red and a toast suggests using a Splitter or a Joiner.
    • 
                        


                        
🚌 Buses — bundle many wires into one

                        
    
                            
  • Join (JOINER): takes N single-bit inputs → one N-bit bus output. Pick the width (2 / 4 / 8 / 16) in the gate header.
    • 
                            
  • Split (SPLITTER): takes one N-bit bus input → N single-bit outputs.
    • 
                            
  • Any standard gate can be widened with the small dropdown in its header (1b, 2b, 4b, 8b, 16b). Widened gates operate bit-by-bit — 4b AND is four AND gates in a trench coat.
    • 
                            
  • Bus wires are drawn thicker and labeled with /N so you can spot them.
    • 
                        


                        
⏱️ Timing gates — Delay, Clock, Pulse

                        
    
                            
  • DELAY: passes its input through after N milliseconds. Great for breaking feedback loops.
    • 
                            
  • CLOCK: toggles its output on and off every N milliseconds. It only runs while its enable input is 1.
    • 
                            
  • PULSE: fires a single-tick high pulse on the rising edge of its input. Perfect for one-shot triggers.
    • 
                        


                        
🎨 Color & Pixel — make it pretty

                        
    
                            
  • Color gate: sets the color of the wire downstream of it. Feed it into a Pixel to paint the pixel that color.
    • 
                            
  • Pixel: a square light you can resize by dragging its bottom-right handle. Great for building small displays.
    • 
                        


                        
📝 Notes — leave yourself a message

                        
    
                            
  • Click 📝 Label to drop a sticky note.
    • 
                            
  • Double-click a note to edit its text.
    • 
                            
  • Drag the bottom-right corner to resize.
    •

    • 
                        


                        
🚑 Quick fixes

                        
    
                            
  • Nothing happens when I click Compile — check the status line under the editor; it'll tell you exactly which line failed.
    • 
                            
  • My custom gate won't save — it needs at least one INPUT/PUSH and at least one OUTPUT that's actually wired up.
    • 
                            
  • My wire is red and flashing — either two outputs are fighting over the same input (remove one), or the wire widths don't match (use a Join or Split).
    • 
                            
  • My clock isn't ticking — feed a 1 into its enable input.
    • 
                            
  • My paste went to the wrong place — Ctrl+V pastes at the cursor; move the cursor first, then paste.
    • 
                            
  • The script panel won't close with Esc — press Esc once to blur the editor, again to close the panel. (Or click ✕.)
    • 
                        


                        
🧭 Still stuck?

    •

    • 
                            
  • The tips box at the bottom-left of the canvas has a running summary of the most useful gestures. Hide it with the ✕ or from Settings → ⚡ Preferences.
